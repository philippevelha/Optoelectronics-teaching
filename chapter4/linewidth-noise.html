Not in the slides. The tables of slides 143 and 149 quote linewidths from 50 kHz to 0.1 nm, and the notes of slide 163 mention “chirping”, but the chapter does not explain what limits the linewidth of a single-mode laser, or why directly modulated lasers change colour. This chapter connects the rate equations (Chapter 12, Chapter 16) with coherence (Ch. 1) and fiber dispersion (Ch. 2).

19.1 Key relations

Linewidth. Even a perfectly single-mode laser is not monochromatic. Each spontaneous photon that enters the lasing mode adds a field of random phase to the coherent field. The phase performs a random walk, and the line acquires a Lorentzian shape whose width is the Schawlow–Townes linewidth, modified for a laser above threshold:

\[ \Delta\nu=\frac{\pi h\nu\,(\Delta\nu_c)^2\,n_{sp}}{P_\text{out}}\,(1+\alpha_H^2),\qquad \Delta\nu_c=\frac{1}{2\pi\tau_{ph}}. \]

The linewidth falls as \(1/P\) and as \(\tau_{ph}^2\): a long, low-loss cavity helps quadratically. In semiconductor lasers the linewidth enhancement factor \(\alpha_H=-\frac{4\pi}{\lambda}\frac{dn/dN}{dg/dN}\approx3\)–6 (Henry, 1982) multiplies the width by \(1+\alpha_H^2\approx10\)–40: every fluctuation of the carrier density changes the refractive index and therefore the phase.

Intensity noise. The same spontaneous events make the power fluctuate. The relative intensity noise (RIN) peaks at the relaxation-oscillation frequency \(f_r\) (Chapter 16) and decreases at high power; its floor is the shot-noise limit \(\text{RIN}_\text{SQL}=2h\nu/P\).

Chirp. When the current changes, the carrier density changes, and with it the index. The optical frequency follows the power transients (Koch and Linke):

\[ \Delta\nu(t)=\frac{\alpha_H}{4\pi}\left[\frac{d\ln P}{dt}+\kappa P\right], \]

where the first term is transient chirp and the second, from gain compression, adiabatic chirp.

19.3 Chirp of a directly modulated laser

Show the Python 
V = 250e-6*5e-6*0.15e-6
t, n, N = lz.ld_step_response(1.1*24e-3, 72e-3, 24e-3, 2.5e-9, 2.16e-12, V, t_end=2e-9, n_t=8000)
k = lz.ld_power(1 + 24e-3, 24e-3, 2.16e-12, 0.32, 3.6, 860e-9, 250e-6)
P = k*lz.qe*V*N/2.16e-12
chirp = 5/(4*np.pi)*np.gradient(np.log(P), t)
fig, ax = plt.subplots(2, 1, figsize=(7.5, 4.6), sharex=True)
ax[0].plot(t*1e9, P*1e3, color=lz.PALETTE[7]); ax[0].set(ylabel="P_o (mW)")
ax[1].plot(t*1e9, chirp/1e9, color=lz.PALETTE[0]); ax[1].set(xlabel="time (ns)", ylabel="Δν (GHz)")
plt.tight_layout(); plt.show()
print(f"peak frequency excursion: {chirp.max()/1e9:.1f} GHz, {-chirp.min()/1e9:.1f} GHz")
peak frequency excursion: 14.8 GHz, 13.0 GHz

Chirp converts the clean intensity pulse into a pulse whose leading and trailing edges have different wavelengths. Fiber dispersion (Ch. 2) then moves them apart. This is why 1550 nm links above 2.5 Gb/s over more than a few tens of km use a CW DFB laser followed by an external modulator (Mach–Zehnder or electro-absorption, Ch. 6), whose chirp is close to zero.

TipGoing further
  • Measure a linewidth. The delayed self-heterodyne method beats the laser with a copy of itself delayed by more than its coherence time and frequency-shifted by an acousto-optic modulator. Simulate it: generate a phase random walk with diffusion coefficient \(2\pi\Delta\nu\), compute the beat spectrum with numpy.fft, and check that it is a Lorentzian of width \(2\Delta\nu\).
  • Narrow-linewidth lasers. Self-injection locking to a high-Q microresonator brings a DFB laser from MHz to Hz-level linewidths. Use the \(\tau_{ph}^2\) scaling to estimate the Q factor needed.
  • Phase noise and coherent communications. QPSK and 16-QAM coherent receivers (Ch. 2) tolerate a linewidth × symbol-time product of about \(10^{-4}\). Which lasers of the calculator are suitable at 32 GBd?

## Chirp of a directly modulated laser

```{python}
#| label: fig-chirp
#| fig-cap: "Power and transient chirp (α_H = 5) of the slide-131 laser switched from 1.1 I_th to 3 I_th. The frequency swings by about ±15 GHz during the relaxation oscillations. In 20 km of standard fiber (D = 17 ps nm⁻¹ km⁻¹), a 10 GHz excursion at 1550 nm (0.08 nm) delays the leading edge relative to the rest by about 27 ps, a quarter of a 10 Gb/s bit."
V = 250e-6*5e-6*0.15e-6
t, n, N = lz.ld_step_response(1.1*24e-3, 72e-3, 24e-3, 2.5e-9, 2.16e-12, V, t_end=2e-9, n_t=8000)
k = lz.ld_power(1 + 24e-3, 24e-3, 2.16e-12, 0.32, 3.6, 860e-9, 250e-6)
P = k*lz.qe*V*N/2.16e-12
chirp = 5/(4*np.pi)*np.gradient(np.log(P), t)
fig, ax = plt.subplots(2, 1, figsize=(7.5, 4.6), sharex=True)
ax[0].plot(t*1e9, P*1e3, color=lz.PALETTE[7]); ax[0].set(ylabel="P_o (mW)")
ax[1].plot(t*1e9, chirp/1e9, color=lz.PALETTE[0]); ax[1].set(xlabel="time (ns)", ylabel="Δν (GHz)")
plt.tight_layout(); plt.show()
print(f"peak frequency excursion: {chirp.max()/1e9:.1f} GHz, {-chirp.min()/1e9:.1f} GHz")
```
