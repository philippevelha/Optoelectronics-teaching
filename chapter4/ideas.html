20.4 Improvements to the course

  1. One equation, three lasers. The threshold condition \(\Gamma g_{th}=\alpha_s+\frac{1}{2L}\ln\frac{1}{R_1R_2}\) and the rate equations explain the He–Ne, the Nd:YAG and the laser diode. The threshold calculator of Chapter 6 puts them side by side: a gain of 0.1 m⁻¹ versus 5000 m⁻¹, photon lifetimes of 30 ns versus 2 ps.
  2. “Predict, then compute.” “If I double the facet reflectance, does the slope efficiency go up or down?”, “Which side of the EDFA gain spectrum disappears first when the inversion drops?”, “Does a Q-switched pulse leave the inversion above or below threshold?”
  3. Fill the missing derivations. Slides 43 (noise figure), 75 (transverse modes) and 88 (mode-locking) state results without the physics; the noise-figure section of Chapter 4 and the two Going further chapters can be used as handouts.
  4. Lab connection. A laser diode on a TEC mount, a power meter and a cheap spectrometer cover threshold, slope efficiency, \(T_0\), mode hops and the mode spacing. A He–Ne laser and a scanning Fabry–Perot show longitudinal and transverse modes.
  5. Modern content. Possible updates: silicon photonics lasers (heterogeneous III–V on Si), quantum-dot and quantum-cascade lasers, frequency combs, high-power fiber lasers, lidar VCSEL arrays, laser safety classes (IEC 60825-1).

20.5 Notes for the instructor: checks on the slides

While recomputing the examples, everything matched the slides (ruby pump power, EDFA example, He–Ne Doppler and threshold examples, laser-diode modes, efficiencies, threshold current and output power, QW and DFB examples) except these points:

  • Slide numbering: the 2024 PDF (Chapter4 - Lasers_2024.pdf, 159 pages) does not follow the 2025 .pptx (170 slides). The companion uses the .pptx numbers.
  • Slide 6: \(dN=4\pi\nu(L/c)^3d\nu\) should be \(4\pi\nu^2(L/c)^3d\nu\) (one polarisation). Slides 6–7 then use \(4\pi k_BT\nu^2/c^3\), while Planck’s law on slide 7 has \(8\pi\): the factor 2 for the two polarisations is missing (Chapter 1).
  • Slide 58: the mode number is printed “1.264,222.5” (i.e. 1,264,222.5).
  • Slide 78: “\(1/\gamma=\tau_2\)” with \(\Delta\nu=2\gamma\) contradicts the field \(E_0e^{-t/2\tau_2}\cos\omega_0t\) on the same slide, which gives \(\Delta\nu=1/(2\pi\tau_2)\), i.e. \(\gamma=1/(4\pi\tau_2)\) (Chapter 7). Slide 80: the normalisation box shows \(\int g_L\) instead of \(\int g_G\).
  • Slide 111: the definition under “Internal Quantum Efficiency” is labelled \(\eta_\text{IDQE}\); the differential efficiency is defined on slide 112.
  • Slide 116: for the 500 mW red laser, \(P_o/(I-I_{th})=500/(700-400)=1.67\) mW/mA, not the 1.0 quoted; all other rows are consistent (Chapter 11).
  • Slide 133: \(n_{th}\approx2.5\times10^{18}\) cm⁻³ for \(g=111\) cm⁻¹ is read from the figure; the curve of slide 130 gives about \(2.4\times10^{18}\) cm⁻³. The results (30 A mm⁻², 37.5 mA) are consistent with 2.5.
  • Slide 139: the finite-well values (0.031, 0.121, 0.007 eV, 848 nm) are about 5 % below a direct solution with the stated parameters (0.033, 0.126, 0.006 eV, 850 nm) (Chapter 13).
  • Slide 143: the footnote “fm is \(10^{-15}\) s” should read \(10^{-15}\) m.
  • Slide 149: for the 680 nm single-mode VCSEL, \(P_o/(I-I_{th})=0.95/2.6=0.37\) mW/mA versus 0.32 quoted (minor).
  • Slide 164, speaker notes: \(t_d=\tau\ln[I_2/(I_2-(I_{th}-I_1))]\) is correct only for \(I_1=0\); the general result is \(t_d=\tau\ln[(I_2-I_1)/(I_2-I_{th})]\) (Chapter 16).
  • Slides 43–44: the noise figure is defined but not derived; the companion adds \(\text{NF}\approx2n_{sp}\) and the ASE power (Section 4.5).
  • Slide 101: the mode spacing uses the phase index (3.6); measured FP spacings follow the group index (\(\approx4.3\) for GaAs), about 20 % smaller (Chapter 10).
