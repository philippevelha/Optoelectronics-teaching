12.1 Key relations

A current \(I\) injects electrons into an active layer of length \(L\), width \(W\) and thickness \(d\) (slide 123). Two rate equations balance them (slide 124):

\[ \frac{I}{eLWd}=\frac{n}{\tau_r}+Cn\,N_\text{ph},\qquad\frac{N_\text{ph}}{\tau_{ph}}=Cn\,N_\text{ph}. \]

Electrons are supplied by the current and removed by spontaneous (\(n/\tau_r\)) and stimulated (\(CnN_\text{ph}\)) recombination. Coherent photons are created by stimulated emission and lost from the cavity with the photon lifetime \(\tau_{ph}\) of Chapter 6. As long as \(N_\text{ph}\neq0\), the second equation fixes the carrier density at

\[ n_{th}=\frac{1}{C\tau_{ph}},\qquad \frac{I_{th}}{eLWd}=\frac{n_{th}}{\tau_r}\qquad\text{(slide 125)}. \]

Above threshold \(n\) stays clamped at \(n_{th}\), and all the extra current becomes photons (slides 125–126):

\[ N_\text{ph}=\frac{\tau_{ph}}{eLWd}(I-I_{th}),\qquad P_o=\left[\frac{hc^2\tau_{ph}(1-R)}{2en\lambda L}\right](I-I_{th}). \]

The threshold density \(n_{th}\) is the density at which the gain reaches threshold. Only the fraction \(\Gamma\) of the mode that overlaps the active layer sees the gain (slide 127), so

\[ \Gamma g_{th}=\alpha_t=\alpha_s+\frac{1}{2L}\ln\frac{1}{R_1R_2}, \]

and \(n_{th}\) follows from the gain curve \(g(n)\) of slide 130 (Chapter 9).

12.3 Worked example (slides 131–134), recomputed

GaAs DH laser at 860 nm: \(L=250\) µm, \(d=0.15\) µm, \(W=5\) µm, \(n=3.6\), \(\alpha_s=10^3\) m⁻¹, \(B=2\times10^{-16}\) m³ s⁻¹. The threshold gain corresponds to \(n_{th}\approx2\times10^{18}\) cm⁻³. Find \(J_{th}\), \(I_{th}\), the output power at \(1.5I_{th}\) and the slope efficiency. How does \(\Gamma=0.5\) change the result?

Show the Python 
L, d, W, n, lam = 250e-6, 0.15e-6, 5e-6, 3.6, 860e-9
R = lz.facet_R(n)
rows = []
for Gam in (1.0, 0.5):
    at = lz.threshold_gain(1e3, L, R)
    gth = at/Gam
    nth_curve = lz.n_for_gain_slide(gth/100)
    nth = (2.0e18 if Gam == 1 else 2.5e18)*lz.CM3              # slide values
    th = lz.ld_threshold(nth, d, L, W, B=2e-16)
    tph = lz.photon_lifetime(at, L, n, exact=False)
    slope = lz.ld_power(th["I_th"] + 1, th["I_th"], tph, R, n, lam, L)
    rows.append({"Γ": Gam, "R": f"{R:.3f}", "g_th (cm⁻¹)": f"{gth/100:.1f}", "n_th from curve (cm⁻³)": f"{nth_curve:.2e}",
                 "n_th used (slide)": f"{nth/lz.CM3:.1e}", "τ_r (ns)": f"{th['tau_r']*1e9:.2f}",
                 "J_th (A/mm²)": f"{th['J_th']/1e6:.1f}", "I_th (mA)": f"{th['I_th']*1e3:.1f}", "τ_ph (ps)": f"{tph*1e12:.2f}",
                 "slope (W/A)": f"{slope:.3f}", "P_o at 1.5 I_th (mW)": f"{slope*0.5*th['I_th']*1e3:.1f}"})
pd.DataFrame(rows).T.rename(columns={0: "Γ = 1 (slides 131–133)", 1: "Γ = 0.5 (slide 133)"})

The slide values are reproduced: \(g_{th}=55.6\) cm⁻¹, \(\tau_r=2.5\) ns, \(J_{th}=19\) A mm⁻², \(I_{th}=24\) mA, \(\tau_{ph}=2.16\) ps, 0.35 W/A and 4.2 mW at 36 mA. With \(\Gamma=0.5\), \(g_{th}=111\) cm⁻¹, \(J_{th}=30\) A mm⁻² and \(I_{th}=37.5\) mA. The digitised curve gives \(n_{th}\approx2.4\times10^{18}\) cm⁻³ for 111 cm⁻¹, close to the 2.5 read on the slide. The slope efficiency does not depend on \(\Gamma\) in this model: above threshold every extra electron still becomes a photon.

12.4 Carrier clamping

from scipy.optimize import brentq
V = L*W*d; tau, tph, beta = 2.5e-9, 2.16e-12, 1e-4
nth = 2e24; C = 1/(nth*tph)
I = np.linspace(0.1e-3, 60e-3, 500)
nn, NN = [], []
for Ii in I:
    f = lambda x: Ii/(lz.qe*V) - x/tau - C*x*(beta*x/tau)/(1/tph - C*x)
    x = brentq(f, 1e10, nth*(1 - 1e-12)); nn.append(x); NN.append(beta*x/tau/(1/tph - C*x))
nn, NN = np.array(nn), np.array(NN)
Pout = 0.5*NN*V*lz.h*lz.c0/lam*(1 - R)*lz.c0/(n*L)               # slide 126: ½ N_ph V hν (1 − R)/(nL/c)
fig, ax = plt.subplots(1, 2, figsize=(11, 3.8))
ax[0].plot(I*1e3, nn/1e24); ax[0].axhline(2, color=lz.INK2, ls="--", lw=1)
ax[0].set(xlabel="current (mA)", ylabel="n (10¹⁸ cm⁻³)")
ax[1].semilogy(I*1e3, Pout*1e3, color=lz.PALETTE[7]); ax[1].set(xlabel="current (mA)", ylabel="P_o per facet (mW, log)")
axr = ax[1].twinx(); axr.plot(I*1e3, Pout*1e3, color=lz.PALETTE[0], lw=1.2); axr.set_ylabel("P_o (mW, linear)", color=lz.PALETTE[0])
axr.spines["right"].set_visible(True); axr.grid(False)
plt.tight_layout(); plt.show()

Above threshold the carrier density, and hence the gain, stays fixed. This is why the gain spectrum does not keep widening with current (slide 129 shows gain spectra at fixed densities, which a lasing diode never exceeds), and why the spontaneous emission saturates at threshold.

TipGoing further
  • Non-radiative recombination (slide 134). Replace \(\tau_r\) by \(\tau\) with \(1/\tau=1/\tau_r+1/\tau_{nr}\), or better use the \(ABC\) model of Chapter 3, \(R(n)=An+Bn^2+Cn^3\): \(I_{th}=eV(An_{th}+Bn_{th}^2+C_\text{Aug}n_{th}^3)\). With \(C_\text{Aug}=7\times10^{-29}\) cm⁶ s⁻¹ (InGaAsP), what fraction of the threshold current is lost to Auger recombination at \(n_{th}=2\times10^{18}\) cm⁻³?
  • Confinement factor of a slab. Compute \(\Gamma\) of the fundamental TE mode of the AlGaAs/GaAs/AlGaAs slab versus \(d\) (Ch. 2 planar waveguide, or the Botez approximation \(\Gamma\approx D^2/(2+D^2)\), \(D=k_0d\sqrt{n_1^2-n_2^2}\)). Combine it with the panel to find the thickness that minimises \(J_{th}\).
  • Gain compression. At high photon density the gain drops, \(g\to g/(1+\epsilon N_\text{ph})\). This damps relaxation oscillations and limits the modulation bandwidth (Chapter 16).

The slide values are reproduced: $g_{th}=55.6$ cm⁻¹, $\tau_r=2.5$ ns, $J_{th}=19$ A mm⁻², $I_{th}=24$ mA, $\tau_{ph}=2.16$ ps,
0.35 W/A and 4.2 mW at 36 mA. With $\Gamma=0.5$, $g_{th}=111$ cm⁻¹, $J_{th}=30$ A mm⁻² and $I_{th}=37.5$ mA. The digitised curve
gives $n_{th}\approx2.4\times10^{18}$ cm⁻³ for 111 cm⁻¹, close to the 2.5 read on the slide. The slope efficiency does not depend
on $\Gamma$ in this model: above threshold every extra electron still becomes a photon.

## Carrier clamping

```{python}
#| label: fig-clamping
#| fig-cap: "Steady state of the rate equations for the slide-131 laser (Γ = 1, τ = τ_r = 2.5 ns, τ_ph = 2.16 ps, spontaneous coupling β = 10⁻⁴). Left: carrier density, clamped at n_th above threshold. Right: output power on a logarithmic (left, red) and a linear (right, blue) axis, showing the spontaneous emission below threshold and the jump at threshold."
from scipy.optimize import brentq
V = L*W*d; tau, tph, beta = 2.5e-9, 2.16e-12, 1e-4
nth = 2e24; C = 1/(nth*tph)
I = np.linspace(0.1e-3, 60e-3, 500)
nn, NN = [], []
for Ii in I:
    f = lambda x: Ii/(lz.qe*V) - x/tau - C*x*(beta*x/tau)/(1/tph - C*x)
    x = brentq(f, 1e10, nth*(1 - 1e-12)); nn.append(x); NN.append(beta*x/tau/(1/tph - C*x))
nn, NN = np.array(nn), np.array(NN)
Pout = 0.5*NN*V*lz.h*lz.c0/lam*(1 - R)*lz.c0/(n*L)               # slide 126: ½ N_ph V hν (1 − R)/(nL/c)
fig, ax = plt.subplots(1, 2, figsize=(11, 3.8))
ax[0].plot(I*1e3, nn/1e24); ax[0].axhline(2, color=lz.INK2, ls="--", lw=1)
ax[0].set(xlabel="current (mA)", ylabel="n (10¹⁸ cm⁻³)")
ax[1].semilogy(I*1e3, Pout*1e3, color=lz.PALETTE[7]); ax[1].set(xlabel="current (mA)", ylabel="P_o per facet (mW, log)")
axr = ax[1].twinx(); axr.plot(I*1e3, Pout*1e3, color=lz.PALETTE[0], lw=1.2); axr.set_ylabel("P_o (mW, linear)", color=lz.PALETTE[0])
axr.spines["right"].set_visible(True); axr.grid(False)
plt.tight_layout(); plt.show()
```

Above threshold the carrier density, and hence the gain, stays fixed. This is why the gain spectrum does not keep widening with
current (slide 129 shows gain spectra at fixed densities, which a lasing diode never exceeds), and why the spontaneous emission saturates at threshold.

## Going further
- **Non-radiative recombination** (slide 134). Replace $\tau_r$ by $\tau$ with $1/\tau=1/\tau_r+1/\tau_{nr}$, or better use the
  $ABC$ model of Chapter 3, $R(n)=An+Bn^2+Cn^3$: $I_{th}=eV(An_{th}+Bn_{th}^2+C_\text{Aug}n_{th}^3)$. With
  $C_\text{Aug}=7\times10^{-29}$ cm⁶ s⁻¹ (InGaAsP), what fraction of the threshold current is lost to Auger recombination at
  $n_{th}=2\times10^{18}$ cm⁻³?
- **Confinement factor of a slab.** Compute $\Gamma$ of the fundamental TE mode of the AlGaAs/GaAs/AlGaAs slab versus $d$ (Ch. 2
  planar waveguide, or the Botez approximation $\Gamma\approx D^2/(2+D^2)$, $D=k_0d\sqrt{n_1^2-n_2^2}$). Combine it with the panel to
  find the thickness that minimises $J_{th}$.
- **Gain compression.** At high photon density the gain drops, $g\to g/(1+\epsilon N_\text{ph})$. This damps relaxation oscillations
  and limits the modulation bandwidth (@sec-modulation).
