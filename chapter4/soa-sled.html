15.1 Key relations

A laser diode below threshold is an amplifier: light that enters one facet leaves the other amplified by the single-pass gain \(G_s=e^{(\Gamma g-\alpha_s)L}\). Two versions exist (slides 155–157):

  • The travelling-wave (TW) SOA has anti-reflection coated (and often angled) facets: one pass, broad and smooth gain spectrum (30–60 nm), about 20–30 dB.
  • The Fabry–Perot (FP) SOA keeps reflecting facets: a regenerative amplifier, whose gain is enhanced at the cavity resonances and suppressed between them, \[G_{FP}=\frac{(1-R_1)(1-R_2)G_s}{\left(1-\sqrt{R_1R_2}\,G_s\right)^2+4\sqrt{R_1R_2}\,G_s\sin^2\phi},\] with \(\phi\) the single-pass phase. The gain ripple is \(\left[(1+\sqrt{R_1R_2}G_s)/(1-\sqrt{R_1R_2}G_s)\right]^2\).

The gain saturates when the signal depletes the carriers (slide 155c). For a homogeneously broadened medium, \(G=G_0\exp[-(G-1)P_\text{in}/P_\text{sat}]\), and the output at which the gain has dropped by 3 dB, \(P_{o,\text{sat}}\), is quoted in datasheets (slide 158). Because the carrier lifetime is ~0.1–1 ns, the SOA gain follows the bit pattern, unlike the EDFA (Chapter 4): this causes cross-talk between WDM channels, but also enables all-optical wavelength conversion.

A superluminescent LED (SLED, slides 159–161) is an SOA with no input: spontaneous emission generated at one end is amplified on its way out, with feedback suppressed by angled facets, an absorbing section or AR coatings. The output is bright and directional like a laser but broadband and incoherent like an LED.

saOut = {
  const R = 10 ** sa.lR, Gs = 10 ** (sa.GsdB/10), rr = R*Gs, fsr = 299792458/(2*3.6*sa.L*1e-6), data = [];

The saturated output power hardly depends on the small-signal gain: it is a property of the medium, \(P_\text{sat}=h\nu A/(\Gamma a\tau)\) with \(a\) the differential gain and \(\tau\) the carrier lifetime. A large mode area and a short lifetime raise it; the 8–11 dBm of slide 158 is typical.

SOAs have NF ≈ 6–7 dB, worse than EDFAs (inversion is never complete, and the input coupling loss adds directly to the NF), and they need hundreds of mW of electrical power. Their advantages are size, any wavelength from 650 to 1650 nm, and integration with lasers and modulators on InP chips.

15.4 Superluminescent LEDs (slides 159–161)

The spontaneous emission generated near the rear of the stripe is amplified along it. Since the gain is largest at the peak, the output spectrum narrows as the gain grows (gain narrowing), but it stays far broader than a laser line.

Show the Python 
lam = np.linspace(1150e-9, 1470e-9, 800)
shape = np.exp(-4*np.log(2)*((lam - 1310e-9)/70e-9)**2)
fig, ax = plt.subplots(figsize=(7.5, 3.6))
for GdB in (0.1, 10, 20, 30):
    gL = GdB/4.343*shape
    P = shape*np.expm1(gL)/np.maximum(gL, 1e-9)
    P /= P.max()
    w = lam[P >= 0.5]; fw = w[-1] - w[0]
    ax.plot(lam*1e9, P, label=f"peak gain {GdB:g} dB: FWHM {fw*1e9:.0f} nm, ℓ_c ≈ {1310e-9**2/fw*1e6:.0f} µm")
ax.set(xlabel="wavelength (nm)", ylabel="normalised power"); ax.legend(fontsize=8); plt.show()

SLEDs are the light source of fiber-optic gyroscopes and of optical coherence tomography (OCT), where the coherence length \(\ell_c\approx\lambda^2/\Delta\lambda\) (Ch. 1) sets the depth resolution: about 10–20 µm for the 40–45 nm SLEDs of slide 159.

## Superluminescent LEDs (slides 159–161)

The spontaneous emission generated near the rear of the stripe is amplified along it. Since the gain is largest at the peak, the
output spectrum narrows as the gain grows (**gain narrowing**), but it stays far broader than a laser line.

```{python}
#| label: fig-sled
#| fig-cap: "Output spectrum of a 1310 nm SLED model: spontaneous emission (Gaussian, 70 nm FWHM) amplified by a gain spectrum of the same shape, P(λ) ∝ r_sp(λ)[G(λ) − 1]/g(λ). Increasing the peak single-pass gain narrows the spectrum; the coherence length λ²/Δλ, which sets the axial resolution of optical coherence tomography, grows accordingly."
lam = np.linspace(1150e-9, 1470e-9, 800)
shape = np.exp(-4*np.log(2)*((lam - 1310e-9)/70e-9)**2)
```
