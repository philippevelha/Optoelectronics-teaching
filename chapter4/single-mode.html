14.1 Key relations

A Fabry–Perot laser diode oscillates on several modes, and hops between them (Chapter 10). WDM systems (Ch. 2) need one wavelength, stable to a fraction of 0.8 nm. The solution is a mirror whose reflectance depends on wavelength: a Bragg grating (Ch. 1, Bragg mirrors; Ch. 2, fiber Bragg gratings). A corrugation of period \(\Lambda\) reflects strongly only near the Bragg wavelength (slide 140b)

\[ \lambda_B=\frac{2n\Lambda}{q},\qquad q=1,2,\dots\ \text{(order)}. \]

  • In a DBR laser (slide 140) the gratings replace the facets, outside the pumped region.
  • In a DFB laser (slides 141–142) the grating runs along the whole active region: feedback is distributed. A uniform grating does not lase at \(\lambda_B\) but on two modes just outside the stop band (slide 142), \[\lambda_m=\lambda_B\pm\frac{\lambda_B^2}{2nL}(m+1).\] An asymmetry (a facet coating, or a quarter-wave phase shift in the middle of the grating) selects one of them.
  • An external cavity laser (slides 145–146) uses an AR-coated diode chip, a lens and an external grating or filter: long cavity, narrow linewidth (kHz), tunable.
  • A VCSEL (slides 147–151) has a cavity about one wavelength long between two epitaxial DBR mirrors. The mode spacing is so large that only one longitudinal mode fits under the gain curve, but the single-pass gain is tiny, so the mirrors need \(R>99\) %.

14.3 Coupled-mode theory of a grating (going further on slides 140–142)

The slide formula treats the grating as a pair of mirrors. Coupled-mode theory (forward and backward waves coupled by the grating with coefficient \(\kappa=\pi\Delta n/\lambda\)) gives the exact response of a uniform grating of length \(L\), with or without gain. Passive, it is the fiber Bragg grating of Ch. 2: a stop band of width \(\Delta\lambda\approx\lambda_B^2\kappa/(\pi n)\) for strong gratings, with peak reflectance \(\tanh^2(\kappa L)\). With gain, the transmission diverges at the frequencies where the structure can oscillate by itself: the DFB laser modes.

14.5 VCSEL mirrors (slides 147–151)

In a VCSEL the light crosses a gain region only a few quantum wells thick, about 30 nm. The single-pass gain is about \(e^{gd}\approx1.003\) for \(g=1000\) cm⁻¹, so the mirrors must lose less than 0.3 % per pass. Epitaxial AlAs/GaAs quarter-wave stacks (Ch. 1, Bragg mirrors) provide this:

Show the Python 
viewof vc = Inputs.form({
  N: Inputs.range([5, 40], {value: 25, step: 1, label: "number of DBR pairs N"}),
  nH: Inputs.range([3.0, 3.7], {value: 3.52, step: 0.01, label: "high index (GaAs)"}),
  nL: Inputs.range([1.5, 3.4], {value: 2.95, step: 0.01, label: "low index (AlAs; ≈ 1.6 for oxidised AlOx)"}),
  dact: Inputs.range([5, 100], {value: 24, step: 1, label: "total QW thickness (nm)"}),
  Gr: Inputs.range([1.0, 2.0], {value: 1.8, step: 0.05, label: "longitudinal enhancement (standing wave)"})
})
Show the Python 
vcOut = {
  const wl0 = 850e-9, spec = [];
  for (let i = 0; i <= 400; i++) { const wl = 700e-9 + 300e-9*i/400; spec.push({wl: wl*1e9, R: dbrSpectrum(wl, vc.N, vc.nH, vc.nL, 1.0, vc.nH, wl0)}); }
  const R = dbrSpectrum(wl0, vc.N, vc.nH, vc.nL, vc.nH, vc.nH, wl0);    // mirror seen from inside the cavity
  const d = vc.dact*1e-9, gth = Math.log(1/(R*R))/(2*d*vc.Gr)/100;      // cm^-1 (internal loss neglected)
  return {spec, R, gth};
}

The 850 nm single-mode VCSEL has \(\delta\lambda=0.2\) pm, that is 83 MHz, and the thresholds are sub-mA: a VCSEL’s active volume is a hundred times smaller than an edge emitter’s. Low cost (wafer-level testing, 2D arrays, slide 150) made VCSELs the source of short-reach datacom links over multimode fiber (Ch. 2) and of face recognition and lidar modules.

TipGoing further
  • Hole burning in DFB lasers. In a λ/4-shifted DFB with large \(\kappa L\) the light is concentrated near the phase shift and depletes the carriers there (“longitudinal spatial hole burning”), which can make the laser jump to a side mode at high power. This sets the optimum \(\kappa L\approx1\)–1.5.
  • Tunable lasers. Sampled-grating DBR lasers combine two combs of reflection peaks with slightly different periods (vernier effect) and tune over 40 nm. Build the two comb spectra numerically and show how a small index change of one comb moves the coincidence by many nm.
  • External-cavity linewidth. The Schawlow–Townes linewidth scales as \(1/\tau_{ph}^2\). Lengthening the cavity from 300 µm (chip) to 3 cm (external) multiplies \(\tau_{ph}\) by about 100 if the losses per pass are similar: estimate the linewidth reduction.
  • VCSEL oxide aperture. Selective oxidation of an AlGaAs layer creates an AlOx aperture (n ≈ 1.6) that confines both current and light, the key to sub-mA thresholds.

[Going further: laser linewidth](linewidth-noise.qmd).

## VCSEL mirrors (slides 147–151)

In a VCSEL the light crosses a gain region only a few quantum wells thick, about 30 nm. The single-pass gain is about
$e^{gd}\approx1.003$ for $g=1000$ cm⁻¹, so the mirrors must lose less than 0.3 % per pass. Epitaxial AlAs/GaAs quarter-wave stacks
(Ch. 1, Bragg mirrors) provide this:

The 850 nm single-mode VCSEL has $\delta\lambda=0.2$ pm, that is 83 MHz, and the thresholds are sub-mA: a VCSEL's active volume is
a hundred times smaller than an edge emitter's. Low cost (wafer-level testing, 2D arrays, slide 150) made VCSELs the source of
short-reach datacom links over multimode fiber (Ch. 2) and of face recognition and lidar modules.

## Going further
- **Hole burning in DFB lasers.** In a λ/4-shifted DFB with large $\kappa L$ the light is concentrated near the phase shift and
  depletes the carriers there ("longitudinal spatial hole burning"), which can make the laser jump to a side mode at high power.
  This sets the optimum $\kappa L\approx1$–1.5.
- **Tunable lasers.** Sampled-grating DBR lasers combine two combs of reflection peaks with slightly different periods (vernier
  effect) and tune over 40 nm. Build the two comb spectra numerically and show how a small index change of one comb moves the
  coincidence by many nm.
- **External-cavity linewidth.** The Schawlow–Townes linewidth scales as $1/\tau_{ph}^2$. Lengthening the cavity from 300 µm (chip)
  to 3 cm (external) multiplies $\tau_{ph}$ by about 100 if the losses per pass are similar: estimate the linewidth reduction.
- **VCSEL oxide aperture.** Selective oxidation of an AlGaAs layer creates an AlOx aperture (n ≈ 1.6) that confines both current and
  light, the key to sub-mA thresholds.
:::
