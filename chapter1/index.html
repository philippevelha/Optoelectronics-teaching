Preface

This companion follows the slides of Chapter 1: The Wave Nature of Light (Basics of Optoelectronics, University of Trento) section by section. The slides tell the story; this book lets you compute it. Every figure is made by a few lines of Python that you can unfold (“Show the Python”), copy and modify. The live panels (marked with a blue bar on the left) recompute directly in your browser. They need no installation.

Chapter 1 gives the toolkit for everything that follows: waveguides and fibers (Ch. 2), LEDs and semiconductor optics (Ch. 3), lasers and amplifiers (Ch. 4), photodetectors (Ch. 5), and polarisation and modulation (Ch. 6). WarningSign convention (read this once)

The slides (following Kasap) use the engineering convention \(E\propto\exp[j(\omega t-kz)]\), which gives a complex index \(N=n-jK\) and a relative permittivity \(\varepsilon_r=\varepsilon_r'-j\varepsilon_r''\). Most physics texts (Born & Wolf, Saleh & Teich, Jackson) use \(\exp[i(kz-\omega t)]\) and \(N=n+i\kappa\). All code here follows the slide convention. Whenever you consult another book, check the convention first: most “wrong sign” errors in Fresnel phases, evanescent fields and Kramers–Kronig relations come from mixing the two.

import optolib as ol
ol.fresnel(1.0, 1.5, 0.3)          # r⊥, r∥, t⊥, t∥ at 0.3 rad
ol.tmm([1, 1.38, 1.52], [100e-9], 550e-9)   # reflectance of a thin-film stack
