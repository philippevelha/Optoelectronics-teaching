2.1 Key relations

When does the external current flow: when the carriers reach the electrodes, or while they move? The Shockley–Ramo theorem answers: while they move. Consider an EHP created at \(x=l\) in a semiconductor of length \(L\) between two electrodes held at a voltage \(V\), with a uniform field \(E=V/L\) (slide 11). In a time \(dt\) the electron drifts \(dx=v_edt\) and the field does the work \(eE\,dx\) on it. That energy comes from the battery, which supplies \(Vi_e(t)dt\) (slide 12):

\[ eE\,dx=Vi_e\,dt\quad\Longrightarrow\quad i_e(t)=\frac{ev_e}{L}\quad (t<t_e),\qquad i_h(t)=\frac{ev_h}{L}\quad (t<t_h), \]

with the transit times \(t_e=(L-l)/v_e\) and \(t_h=l/v_h\) (slide 14). Each carrier gives a rectangular current pulse. Neither pulse alone carries a full electron charge, but together they do (slide 15):

\[ Q=\int_0^{t_e}i_e\,dt+\int_0^{t_h}i_h\,dt=e\frac{L-l}{L}+e\frac{l}{L}=e . \]

The current starts as soon as the pair is created, not when the first carrier arrives. The duration of the pulse, set by the slowest carrier that has the longest way to go, is the transit-time limit of the detector speed (Chapter 5).

rmOut = {
  const L = rm.L * 1e-6, E = rm.V / L;
  const ve = rm.sat ? driftVelocity(E, "e") : 1e5, vh = rm.sat ? driftVelocity(E, "h") : 5e4;

  return {pts, E, ve, vh, te, th, Qe: (1 - rm.l), Qh: rm.l};
}

2.3 Many pairs: the transit-time impulse response

A short light pulse creates pairs with the profile \(e^{-\alpha x}\) of Chapter 3 (light entering at \(x=0\), on the side where the holes are collected). The photocurrent is the sum of the rectangular pulses of all pairs. Its Fourier transform is the transit-time frequency response.

Strong absorption (\(\alpha W\gg1\)) creates all pairs near \(x=0\): only electrons cross the device, and the pulse lasts \(W/v_e\). Weak absorption gives uniform generation: both carriers contribute, each pulse is a triangle. Because the holes are slower, it pays to generate the carriers on the side where the holes are collected. This is why fast photodiodes are lit through the p⁺ side, and why the “uni-travelling-carrier” photodiode (see Going further) lets only electrons travel.

TipGoing further
  • The general theorem. For any electrode geometry, a charge \(q\) moving with velocity \(\mathbf v\) induces on electrode \(k\) the current \(i_k=q\,\mathbf v\cdot\mathbf E_w\), where \(\mathbf E_w\) is the weighting field: the field obtained with electrode \(k\) at 1 V, all others grounded and all charges removed. For parallel plates \(E_w=1/L\) and we recover slide 12. In an interdigitated MSM detector (Chapter 7) \(E_w\) is concentrated near the fingers: a carrier contributes most of its current when it is close to the electrode. Compute \(E_w\) for a pixelated X-ray detector and explain the “small-pixel effect”.
  • Uni-travelling-carrier (UTC) photodiode. Absorb the light in a thin p-doped layer, where the photogenerated holes are majority carriers and relax immediately; only electrons enter the depleted collector, at velocities up to \(3\times10^5\) m/s in InP (velocity overshoot). UTC photodiodes reach > 100 GHz and high saturation currents (no hole pile-up). Use the panel with the hole velocity set very high to mimic it.
  • Space charge. At high photocurrents the drifting carriers screen the field (the photocurrent density approaches \(\varepsilon v_{sat}V/W^2\)) and the response saturates. Estimate this saturation current for a 20 µm diameter, 1 µm thick InGaAs pin at 3 V.

```{python}
L, l, V = 10e-6, 3e-6, 10.0
ve, vh = dl.drift_velocity(V/L, "e"), dl.drift_velocity(V/L, "h")
t = np.linspace(0, 2e-10, 200001)
ie, ih = dl.ramo_single(t, l, L, ve, vh)
Qe, Qh = np.trapezoid(ie, t), np.trapezoid(ih, t)
pd.DataFrame([("field E = V/L", f"{V/L:.1e} V/m"), ("v_e, v_h (Caughey–Thomas)", f"{ve:.3g}, {vh:.3g} m/s"),
              ("t_e = (L − l)/v_e", f"{(L - l)/ve*1e12:.1f} ps"), ("t_h = l/v_h", f"{l/vh*1e12:.1f} ps"),
              ("i_e = ev_e/L, i_h = ev_h/L", f"{dl.qe*ve/L*1e9:.2f} nA, {dl.qe*vh/L*1e9:.2f} nA"),
              ("charge Q_e + Q_h", f"{Qe/dl.qe:.3f} e + {Qh/dl.qe:.3f} e = {(Qe + Qh)/dl.qe:.3f} e")],
             columns=["quantity", "value"])
```

## Going further
- **The general theorem.** For any electrode geometry, a charge $q$ moving with velocity $\mathbf v$ induces on electrode $k$ the
  current $i_k=q\,\mathbf v\cdot\mathbf E_w$, where $\mathbf E_w$ is the **weighting field**: the field obtained with electrode $k$
  at 1 V, all others grounded and all charges removed. For parallel plates $E_w=1/L$ and we recover slide 12. In an interdigitated
  MSM detector (@sec-schottky) $E_w$ is concentrated near the fingers: a carrier contributes most of its current when it is close to
  the electrode. Compute $E_w$ for a pixelated X-ray detector and explain the "small-pixel effect".
- **Uni-travelling-carrier (UTC) photodiode.** Absorb the light in a thin p-doped layer, where the photogenerated holes are majority
  carriers and relax immediately; only electrons enter the depleted collector, at velocities up to $3\times10^5$ m/s in InP
  (velocity overshoot). UTC photodiodes reach > 100 GHz and high saturation currents (no hole pile-up). Use the panel with the hole
  velocity set very high to mimic it.
- **Space charge.** At high photocurrents the drifting carriers screen the field (the photocurrent density approaches
  $\varepsilon v_{sat}V/W^2$) and the response saturates. Estimate this saturation current for a 20 µm diameter, 1 µm thick InGaAs
  pin at 3 V.
:::
