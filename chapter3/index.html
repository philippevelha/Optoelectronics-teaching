Preface

This companion follows the slides of Chapter 3: Semiconductor Science and Light-Emitting Diodes (Basics of Optoelectronics, University of Trento) section by section. The slides tell the story; this book lets you compute it. Every figure is made by a few lines of Python that you can unfold (“Show the Python”), copy and modify. The live panels (marked with a blue bar on the left) recompute directly in your browser. They need no installation.

 Interactive explorers in Google Colab (no installation, Google account needed).

Where this chapter sits in the course

Chapter 1 described light as a wave and Chapter 2 guided it in fibers. Chapter 3 builds the source. It starts from the band picture of a crystal, counts electrons and holes, assembles a pn junction, and ends with the light-emitting diode: its spectrum, its efficiency and how fast it can be switched. The same physics (bands, quasi-Fermi levels, injection, recombination) is the basis of laser diodes (Ch. 4) and photodetectors (Ch. 5), which are pn junctions run in the other direction.

NoteUnits in the code

The slides quote concentrations in cm\(^{-3}\) and mobilities in cm\(^2\) V\(^{-1}\) s\(^{-1}\), as most semiconductor texts do. The code works in SI units (m\(^{-3}\), m\(^2\) V\(^{-1}\) s\(^{-1}\)) to avoid factor-of-\(10^6\) mistakes in \(\varepsilon\), \(e\) and \(h\). Energies are in eV. Two constants convert: sl.CM3 = 1e6 (cm\(^{-3}\) → m\(^{-3}\)) and sl.CM2 = 1e-4 (cm\(^2\) → m\(^2\)). At 300 K, \(k_BT = 25.85\) meV.

Using the code

All functions live in a small, readable module, semilib.py, which you can import from your own notebooks:

import semilib as sl
sl.carriers(1e16*sl.CM3, 0, sl.MATERIALS["Si"]["ni"])   # n, p of n-type Si (m^-3)
sl.qw_finite(12e-9, 0.30, 0.067)                        # bound levels of a GaAs quantum well (eV)
