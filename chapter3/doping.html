3.1 Key relations

A group-V atom (As, P) in Si gives a donor level a few tens of meV below \(E_c\); at room temperature it is ionised and \(n\approx N_d\). A group-III atom (B) gives an acceptor just above \(E_v\) and \(p\approx N_a\) (slides 19–23). The minority concentration follows from the mass action law. With both dopants present (compensation, slides 26–27):

\[ N_d>N_a:\ \ n=N_d-N_a,\ \ p=\frac{n_i^2}{N_d-N_a};\qquad N_a>N_d:\ \ p=N_a-N_d,\ \ n=\frac{n_i^2}{N_a-N_d}. \]

The Fermi level moves away from \(E_{Fi}\) by (slides 29–30)

\[ E_{Fn}-E_{Fi}=k_BT\ln\frac{n}{n_i},\qquad E_{Fp}-E_{Fi}=-k_BT\ln\frac{p}{n_i}. \]

Carriers drift in a field with \(v_{de}=\mu_eE_x\), \(v_{dh}=\mu_hE_x\), and the conductivity is (slide 18)

\[ \sigma=en\mu_e+ep\mu_h . \]

The code solves the exact neutrality condition \(n+N_a=p+N_d\) with \(np=n_i^2\) (sl.carriers). It reduces to the formulas above whenever the net doping is much larger than \(n_i\), and it also handles the intermediate cases.

3.4 Where is the Fermi level?

Show the Python 
N = np.logspace(10, 20, 300)*CM3
kT = sl.kT()
EFi = Si["Eg"]/2 - 0.5*kT*np.log(Si["Nc"]/Si["Nv"])
# exact (Fermi–Dirac) position: solve Nc F1/2((EF−Ec)/kT) − Nv F1/2((Ev−EF)/kT) = Nd − Na
def EF_exact(Nnet):
    f = lambda EF: (Si["Nc"]*sl.fermi_half((EF - Si["Eg"])/kT) - Si["Nv"]*sl.fermi_half(-EF/kT) - Nnet)/abs(Nnet)
    return brentq(f, -0.4, Si["Eg"] + 0.4)
Nn = np.logspace(14, 20, 25)*CM3
fig, ax = plt.subplots()
ax.semilogx(N/CM3, EFi + kT*np.log(sl.carriers(N, 0, Si["ni"])[0]/Si["ni"]), "--", color=sl.PALETTE[0], lw=1.4, label="n-type, Boltzmann")
ax.semilogx(N/CM3, EFi - kT*np.log(sl.carriers(0, N, Si["ni"])[1]/Si["ni"]), "--", color=sl.PALETTE[1], lw=1.4, label="p-type, Boltzmann")
ax.semilogx(Nn/CM3, [EF_exact(x) for x in Nn], color=sl.PALETTE[0], label="n-type, Fermi–Dirac")
ax.semilogx(Nn/CM3, [EF_exact(-x) for x in Nn], color=sl.PALETTE[1], label="p-type, Fermi–Dirac")
ax.axhspan(Si["Eg"] - 3*kT, Si["Eg"] + 0.1, color=sl.PALETTE[0], alpha=0.08); ax.axhspan(-0.1, 3*kT, color=sl.PALETTE[1], alpha=0.08)
for y, t in ((Si["Eg"], "E_c"), (0, "E_v"), (EFi, "E_Fi")):

3.5 Mobility depends on doping

The constant mobilities of Table 3.1 hold for lightly doped crystals. Ionised dopants scatter carriers, and the empirical Caughey–Thomas law describes the drop:

\[ \mu(N)=\mu_\text{min}+\frac{\mu_\text{max}-\mu_\text{min}}{1+(N/N_\text{ref})^\alpha}. \]

Show the Python 
def mu_ct(N, carrier):
    mmin, mmax, Nref, a = (68.5, 1414, 9.2e16, 0.711) if carrier == "e" else (44.9, 470.5, 2.23e17, 0.719)
    return mmin + (mmax - mmin)/(1 + (N/Nref)**a)      # N in cm^-3, result in cm²/Vs
N = np.logspace(13, 20, 300)
fig, ax = plt.subplots(1, 2, figsize=(9.5, 3.8))
ax[0].semilogx(N, mu_ct(N, "e"), label="electrons"); ax[0].semilogx(N, mu_ct(N, "h"), label="holes")
ax[0].plot(1e16, 1200, "o", color=sl.PALETTE[7], ms=7); ax[0].text(1.3e16, 1230, "slide 31", fontsize=8)
ax[0].set(xlabel="dopant concentration (cm⁻³)", ylabel="μ (cm² V⁻¹ s⁻¹)"); ax[0].legend()
ax[1].loglog(N, 1/(sl.qe*N*mu_ct(N, "e")), label="n-type"); ax[1].loglog(N, 1/(sl.qe*N*mu_ct(N, "h")), label="p-type")
ax[1].set(xlabel="dopant concentration (cm⁻³)", ylabel="resistivity ρ (Ω cm)"); ax[1].legend()
plt.tight_layout(); plt.show()

3.6 Beyond the slides: freeze-out and the intrinsic regime

“All donors are ionised” fails at low temperature, where electrons fall back onto the donors (freeze-out). It also fails at high temperature, where \(n_i\) overtakes \(N_d\). Solving neutrality with the ionised-donor fraction \(N_d^+=N_d/[1+2\exp((E_F-E_d)/k_BT)]\) shows the three regimes of every doped semiconductor:

Show the Python 
Nd, Ed = 1e15*CM3, 0.045
T = np.linspace(25, 900, 300)
n_out = []
for Tk in T:
    kt = sl.kT(Tk)
    Nc, Nv = Si["Nc"]*(Tk/300)**1.5, Si["Nv"]*(Tk/300)**1.5
    Eg = sl.varshni(Tk, *sl.VARSHNI["Si"]) - sl.varshni(300, *sl.VARSHNI["Si"]) + Si["Eg"]
    def neutral(x):                              # x = (Ec − EF)/kT
        n = Nc*np.exp(-x); p = Nv*np.exp(-(Eg/kt - x)); Ndp = Nd/(1 + 2*np.exp(Ed/kt - x))
        return np.log(n + 1e-300) - np.log(p + Ndp)
    x = brentq(neutral, -5, Eg/kt + 5)
    n_out.append(Nc*np.exp(-x))
n_out = np.array(n_out)
fig, ax = plt.subplots()
ax.semilogy(T, n_out/CM3, label="n (exact neutrality)")
ax.semilogy(T, sl.n_i("Si", T)/CM3, "--", color=sl.INK2, lw=1.3, label="n_i")
ax.axhline(Nd/CM3, color=sl.PALETTE[1], ls=":", lw=1.3, label="N_d")
ax.set(xlabel="T (K)", ylabel="n (cm⁻³)", ylim=(1e11, 1e17)); ax.legend(); plt.show()

3.7 Bands tilt in an applied field (slide 32)

A voltage \(V\) across a bar of length \(L\) adds the potential energy \(-eV(x)\) to every electron: the whole band diagram tilts with slope \(eE_x\). Electrons roll “downhill” in the CB, holes “uphill” in the VB, and the Fermi levels at the two contacts differ by \(eV\), exactly the rule of slide 17. A 1 cm Si bar with 1 V applied tilts by 1 eV over 1 cm. The same picture gives the band bending inside a pn junction (Chapter 4), where the field comes from the space charge.

## Where is the Fermi level?

```{python}
#| label: fig-ef-doping
#| fig-cap: "Fermi level position in Si at 300 K versus net doping. Dashed: Boltzmann formula E_F − E_Fi = ±kT ln(N/n_i). The shaded bands mark where E_F comes within 3kT of a band edge: the material becomes degenerate (slide 28) and the formula overestimates the shift."
N = np.logspace(10, 20, 300)*CM3
kT = sl.kT()
EFi = Si["Eg"]/2 - 0.5*kT*np.log(Si["Nc"]/Si["Nv"])
# exact (Fermi–Dirac) position: solve Nc F1/2((EF−Ec)/kT) − Nv F1/2((Ev−EF)/kT) = Nd − Na
def EF_exact(Nnet):
    f = lambda EF: (Si["Nc"]*sl.fermi_half((EF - Si["Eg"])/kT) - Si["Nv"]*sl.fermi_half(-EF/kT) - Nnet)/abs(Nnet)
    return brentq(f, -0.4, Si["Eg"] + 0.4)
Nn = np.logspace(14, 20, 25)*CM3
fig, ax = plt.subplots()
ax.semilogx(N/CM3, EFi + kT*np.log(sl.carriers(N, 0, Si["ni"])[0]/Si["ni"]), "--", color=sl.PALETTE[0], lw=1.4, label="n-type, Boltzmann")
ax.semilogx(N/CM3, EFi - kT*np.log(sl.carriers(0, N, Si["ni"])[1]/Si["ni"]), "--", color=sl.PALETTE[1], lw=1.4, label="p-type, Boltzmann")
ax.semilogx(Nn/CM3, [EF_exact(x) for x in Nn], color=sl.PALETTE[0], label="n-type, Fermi–Dirac")
ax.semilogx(Nn/CM3, [EF_exact(-x) for x in Nn], color=sl.PALETTE[1], label="p-type, Fermi–Dirac")
ax.axhspan(Si["Eg"] - 3*kT, Si["Eg"] + 0.1, color=sl.PALETTE[0], alpha=0.08); ax.axhspan(-0.1, 3*kT, color=sl.PALETTE[1], alpha=0.08)
for y, t in ((Si["Eg"], "E_c"), (0, "E_v"), (EFi, "E_Fi")):
    ax.axhline(y, color=sl.INK if t != "E_Fi" else sl.INK2, lw=1 if t != "E_Fi" else 0.8, ls="-" if t != "E_Fi" else ":")
    ax.text(1.2e10, y + 0.015, t, fontsize=8)
ax.set(xlabel="dopant concentration (cm⁻³)", ylabel="E_F − E_v (eV)", ylim=(-0.1, 1.2)); ax.legend(fontsize=8, loc="center left")
plt.show()
```

## Mobility depends on doping

The constant mobilities of Table 3.1 hold for lightly doped crystals. Ionised dopants scatter carriers, and the empirical
Caughey–Thomas law describes the drop:

$$
\mu(N)=\mu_\text{min}+\frac{\mu_\text{max}-\mu_\text{min}}{1+(N/N_\text{ref})^\alpha}.
$$

```{python}
#| label: fig-mobility
#| fig-cap: "Si at 300 K: drift mobility (left) and resistivity (right) versus doping (Caughey–Thomas parameters of Masetti/Arora). The slide-31 value μe ≈ 1200 cm²/Vs at 10¹⁶ cm⁻³ is marked."
def mu_ct(N, carrier):
    mmin, mmax, Nref, a = (68.5, 1414, 9.2e16, 0.711) if carrier == "e" else (44.9, 470.5, 2.23e17, 0.719)
    return mmin + (mmax - mmin)/(1 + (N/Nref)**a)      # N in cm^-3, result in cm²/Vs
N = np.logspace(13, 20, 300)
fig, ax = plt.subplots(1, 2, figsize=(9.5, 3.8))
ax[0].semilogx(N, mu_ct(N, "e"), label="electrons"); ax[0].semilogx(N, mu_ct(N, "h"), label="holes")
ax[0].plot(1e16, 1200, "o", color=sl.PALETTE[7], ms=7); ax[0].text(1.3e16, 1230, "slide 31", fontsize=8)
ax[0].set(xlabel="dopant concentration (cm⁻³)", ylabel="μ (cm² V⁻¹ s⁻¹)"); ax[0].legend()
ax[1].loglog(N, 1/(sl.qe*N*mu_ct(N, "e")), label="n-type"); ax[1].loglog(N, 1/(sl.qe*N*mu_ct(N, "h")), label="p-type")
ax[1].set(xlabel="dopant concentration (cm⁻³)", ylabel="resistivity ρ (Ω cm)"); ax[1].legend()
plt.tight_layout(); plt.show()
print(f"μh at 2e17 cm⁻³: {mu_ct(2e17, 'h'):.0f} cm²/Vs (Table 3.1: 490); μe at 1e16: {mu_ct(1e16, 'e'):.0f} cm²/Vs")
```

## Beyond the slides: freeze-out and the intrinsic regime

"All donors are ionised" fails at low temperature, where electrons fall back onto the donors (**freeze-out**). It also fails at
high temperature, where $n_i$ overtakes $N_d$. Solving neutrality with the ionised-donor fraction
$N_d^+=N_d/[1+2\exp((E_F-E_d)/k_BT)]$ shows the three regimes of every doped semiconductor:

```{python}
#| label: fig-freezeout
#| fig-cap: "Electron concentration of Si doped with 10¹⁵ cm⁻³ phosphorus (E_c − E_d = 45 meV) versus temperature. Ionisation (freeze-out), extrinsic plateau n ≈ N_d, and intrinsic regime n ≈ n_i. Devices are designed to work on the plateau."
Nd, Ed = 1e15*CM3, 0.045
T = np.linspace(25, 900, 300)
n_out = []
for Tk in T:
    kt = sl.kT(Tk)
    Nc, Nv = Si["Nc"]*(Tk/300)**1.5, Si["Nv"]*(Tk/300)**1.5
    Eg = sl.varshni(Tk, *sl.VARSHNI["Si"]) - sl.varshni(300, *sl.VARSHNI["Si"]) + Si["Eg"]
    def neutral(x):                              # x = (Ec − EF)/kT
        n = Nc*np.exp(-x); p = Nv*np.exp(-(Eg/kt - x)); Ndp = Nd/(1 + 2*np.exp(Ed/kt - x))
        return np.log(n + 1e-300) - np.log(p + Ndp)
    x = brentq(neutral, -5, Eg/kt + 5)
    n_out.append(Nc*np.exp(-x))
n_out = np.array(n_out)
fig, ax = plt.subplots()
ax.semilogy(T, n_out/CM3, label="n (exact neutrality)")
ax.semilogy(T, sl.n_i("Si", T)/CM3, "--", color=sl.INK2, lw=1.3, label="n_i")
ax.axhline(Nd/CM3, color=sl.PALETTE[1], ls=":", lw=1.3, label="N_d")
ax.set(xlabel="T (K)", ylabel="n (cm⁻³)", ylim=(1e11, 1e17)); ax.legend(); plt.show()
```

## Bands tilt in an applied field (slide 32)

A voltage $V$ across a bar of length $L$ adds the potential energy $-eV(x)$ to every electron: the whole band diagram tilts with
slope $eE_x$. Electrons roll "downhill" in the CB, holes "uphill" in the VB, and the Fermi levels at the two contacts differ
by $eV$, exactly the rule of slide 17. A 1 cm Si bar with 1 V applied tilts by 1 eV over 1 cm. The same
picture gives the band bending inside a pn junction (@sec-junction), where the field comes from the space charge.
